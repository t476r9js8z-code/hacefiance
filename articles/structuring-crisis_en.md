# The Structuring Crisis

*A subject from the Hacéfiance constellation — September 2026. CC0 licence.*

---

## Flux as a Condition

A dissipative structure is not born in stillness. It requires a departure from equilibrium and a sustained input: this is the condition Prigogine set out, and it holds well beyond chemistry. Wherever a flux crosses a medium, forms appear that facilitate that flux.

A crisis is such an input. It concentrates, within weeks, an intensity of attention, resources and decisional urgency that an ordinary regime never reaches. What this input produces is not an intensification of the prior state: it is a change of phase.

Speed is the marker. Forty-five days separate 11 September 2001 from the enactment of the Patriot Act; an entire federal department is created in its wake. Nothing in the ordinary regime produces timelines of that kind, and this is precisely what indicates that the regime has changed. The entry into war in 1941 and the health crisis of 2020 show the same temporal signature.

Before, influence between organisations operates as a field — diffuse, stochastic, with no identifiable carrier, and at near-zero maintenance cost since it is the state of least constraint. One cannot audit a gradient. After, the same influence is inscribed: procedures, obligations, precedents, supply contracts, indemnification clauses. It has become more efficient, and for the first time it has a surface.

This is the first result, and it is counter-intuitive. **A crisis does not conceal what is being put in place. It forces it to take form, and thereby makes it documentable.**

## The Complex

What forms under flux, when several distinct organisations find themselves aligned by the same urgency, deserves a name of its own. Not a coalition, which presupposes intent, nor a conspiracy, which presupposes a centre — but a **complex**: a junction of interests become structural, capable of exerting weight without anyone directing it. Eisenhower already used the word in 1961 in exactly this sense, and his warning bore on the absence of a pilot.

Three conditions suffice for its formation:

- **aligned incentives** — each party has an interest in the same outcome, for different reasons;
- **welded interfaces** — under time pressure, mutual verification becomes a luxury and cross-checks are suspended;
- **an operative constraint** — a mechanism by which one actor's decision becomes another's obligation.

The third is decisive, and it is the one most often omitted. So long as the parties remain distinct, they partially check one another. Placed in series by a constraint, each becomes dependent on the others holding.

The health crisis of 2020-2022 offers the most complete demonstration, because all four storeys are separately documented: a product developed under emergency conditions; advance purchase agreements transferring liability risk to states; regulatory agencies whose funding depends chiefly on fees from the industry they assess; administrative apparatuses charged with enforcing obligations; and a political class publicly committed to the outcome.

The lock closes at the moment the obligation comes into play. The product becomes a constraint, the constraint becomes administrative enforcement, enforcement becomes a political position to defend. From there, any one party's retraction exposes the others. The lock is mechanical and requires no one's assent.

The post-2001 security complex shows the same serial arrangement, with an intelligence product in place of a pharmaceutical one. It is the closest comparator, term for term, and it has a twenty-year head start: what it has become indicates what a complex becomes when it is not examined.

## Self-Feeding

A dissipative structure cannot merely subsist. It must keep importing. Deprived of flux, it does not return to the prior state: it degrades. This asymmetry of return is what makes the sequel predictable.

The flux itself must be identified. For an institutional complex it is neither the budget nor the mandate: it is **adherence**. From the system's standpoint its origin is indifferent — spontaneous, obtained by constraint, or produced by fear and the social cost of dissent, it supplies the same throughput. What matters is not its quality but that it enters.

Hence a constraint that the acute regime conceals. Adherence produced by forcing is costly to produce: obligations to administer, exemptions to process, dissent to disqualify. The system spends in order to capture its own flux, and that yield is sustainable only while the emergency covers the price.

Routinisation is not a replacement source. Perpetuated budgets, standing preparedness mandates, emergency provisions folded into ordinary law, procedures written into internal regulations: this is the conversion of a past flux into durable form. Stored energy, not incoming energy. It conserves; it does not feed.

The handling of contestation constitutes the only residual intake. Each objection justifies the apparatus that processes it; each discreditable criticism reinforces the need to sort the legitimate from the illegitimate. The throughput is small but continuous, and it suffices to maintain a compliance apparatus long after everything else has stopped.

When the forcing ends, throughput falls. The complex does not dissolve for all that, and it no longer grows: **it freezes**. This is the finished form — a structure that has ceased to dissipate and retains exactly the configuration acquired during the period of input.

The post-2001 case shows this at full scale. Public adherence to surveillance measures collapses after 2013, and the measures do not retreat: successive renewals instead complete the transfer of the exception into ordinary law. The fall in flux did not undo the structure; it only halted its growth.

Freezing has a consequence dissolution would not have had. A structure that no longer consumes can no longer correct itself either, since correction likewise requires energy. It therefore keeps intact its precedents, its instruments, and the authority displaced during the crisis — inert, available, and reactivable at the first subsequent input without needing to be rebuilt.

## The Window

Between the end of the initial input and the completion of routinisation there opens a period in which the structure still costs something to maintain without having merged into the background. It is the only period in which it remains measurable by difference: it has a before and an after that have not yet fused.

The 1961 address is the exact illustration. Eisenhower speaks some fifteen years after the structuring crisis, at the precise moment routinisation is completing — late enough for the form to be visible, early enough that it is not yet the scenery. No one could have delivered that speech in 1975: there would no longer have been a term of comparison.

The window closes by simple passage of time, as those who knew the prior state cease to be those who decide. An order installed during a dated event, before witnesses, remains discontinuous for roughly a generation.

This is what gives public examination its weight. An inquiry into a crisis episode does not correct the transition: it fixes the point of comparison before it becomes inaccessible. Its absence is therefore not a neutral abstention — it is the operation that lets the window close. In energetic terms, the refusal to examine is intelligible without invoking bad faith: an examination forces expenditure at the very moment the structure can no longer afford it.

## Loss of Scale Relation

Here is the central mechanism.

In a hierarchy of complexity, scales nest: they do not replace one another. A part draws its meaning from the relation it maintains with the level that contains it, and that relation is what allows one to measure what it costs against what it serves.

A self-feeding complex loses that relation. Not because it rebels, but because its measure becomes internal. Its indicators are its own, its success criteria are produced by it, its justification circulates in a closed loop. It no longer perceives itself as a component: it treats its own scale as the whole.

The symptom is Goodhart's law generalised. Once a measure becomes the target, it ceases to measure.

Aviation security offers the clearest case, because the gap there is quantified on both sides. The apparatus is assessed on indicators of compliance and throughput — items confiscated, passengers processed, procedures followed — never on the function of which those indicators were the trace. Internal audits establishing massive failure rates in penetration testing did not produce redesign: they produced procedural responses. A coupled component would have treated those audits as a signal from the whole; a decoupled component treats them as an internal compliance problem.

The same operation reads elsewhere under other indicators: seizure counts and confiscated value in the war on drugs, coverage rates during the health crisis. In each case the trace substitutes itself for the function, and the operation is carried out in complete sincerity. That is exactly what makes it hard to interrupt from within.

## Capture and Counterfeit

Two corollaries follow, and they change the status of decoupling.

**Decoupling is a capture.** A component that must sustain itself does not merely ignore the whole: it captures its flux. The cell that no longer hears the organism does not merely divide — it recruits a blood supply.

The war on drugs gives the literal form. Civil asset forfeiture allows the apparatus to feed directly on what it seizes: the component's funding becomes a function of its activity, independent of the result obtained for the whole. This is no longer a thermodynamic analogy but a supply circuit in the proper sense — which is why this case is the best test bench for the notion.

**The compass is counterfeitable.** The most effective failure mode is not to break with the whole: it is to borrow its vocabulary. A decoupled component never announces its autonomy; it declares that it serves the whole — and declares it sincerely, since its internal measure confirms as much. An orientation signal that can be imitated no longer orients anything.

A requirement of form follows. The compass is not an asserted value but an **infrastructure**: a channel through which an injured party can signal to the whole that it is injured, and a body of appeal external to whoever decides. An arrangement whose assessment is anonymous and whose appeal is internal has no compass, whatever values it proclaims.

## Ablation of the Receptors

A component under maintenance pressure treats a contradictory signal as a load. The cheapest way to reduce that load is not to answer it: it is to disqualify the sender. The operation is inexpensive, locally rational, and requires no malice.

It is, however, irreversible in its effects. A component that removes its receptors can no longer correct itself, even should it later wish to. And the operation is self-validating: the excluded party hardens, lacking any space in which one is corrected among peers; that hardening retroactively confirms the exclusion; the loop closes without either party having chosen it.

The shift of register is the tell. After 2001, internal reports on the legality of surveillance programmes were handled through criminal proceedings rather than examination of their content. During the health crisis, researchers and clinicians were sanctioned by universities and professional bodies for public statements on scientific matters — and the fact that arbitration bodies subsequently reduced several of those sanctions on academic-freedom grounds confirms the diagnosis: the treatment was administrative where the question was epistemic.

This is the only point in the entire process that engages individual responsibility. Capture is structural; exclusion is an act.

## Inversion

Ablation prepares a deeper displacement, which is no longer a decoupling but an **inversion**: within a single scale, authority separates from competence. The less informed layer acquires the right to rule for the more informed one.

Ashby supplies the formal ground. The law of requisite variety holds that a regulator must possess at least the variety of the system it regulates. A human resources department, a vice-rectorate, a professional body are compliance apparatuses: they process non-conformities, they do not weigh evidence. Their variety is several orders below that of the domain on which they rule. What they exercise is therefore not regulation — it is a reduction of the regulated to whatever the regulator knows how to process.

Research ethics committees offer the clearest illustration, and the least polemical because it depends on no crisis episode. Created for a real and bounded problem — the consent of human subjects — they today rule on the methodological quality of protocols they lack the variety to evaluate. The extension occurred without decision, by accumulation of precedent, and no one ever asked for it.

Two mechanisms make inversion stable. Responsibility evaporates along the chain: each level applies a directive originating elsewhere and no one owns the whole decision. And local zeal is free: over-applying an instruction costs nothing, qualifying it exposes you. The harshest applications therefore occur at intermediate levels, those with neither the mandate nor the information to arbitrate.

Inversion outlives governments. What administrative apparatuses retain from a crisis episode is that an emergency justification suspends internal debate and that excess zeal carries no cost. That learning stays inscribed in the procedures, available for the next time.

## The Criterion

Distinguishing a coupled component from a decoupled one can be done without recourse to judgement.

> A component still coupled is subsidised by the whole, because it serves it: its maintenance cost falls relative to its contribution. A decoupled component must extract its energy against the whole: its maintenance cost rises while its contribution falls.

The criterion has three properties that make it operative.

It **requires no access to intentions**: it bears on flows, not motives, and therefore works where moral judgement stops.

It is **computable**: two series suffice — what the structure consumes, what it produces for the level that contains it — and their ratio can be tracked over time.

It is **refutable**, which is its condition of usefulness: a structure whose maintenance cost rises durably while manifestly remaining in the service of the whole would suffice to invalidate it.

The regulatory lock following Three Mile Island is the best test bench, because both series there are public and long. The cost and duration of the licensing process rise continuously, while new construction starts halt for close to three decades. The ratio therefore degrades in the criterion's sense.

And this is where the possible refutation must be read, because it is a serious one. A safety regulator's real contribution is not authorised capacity: it is the accident that does not occur. An invisible contribution enters no series. Moreover, nuclear output itself continued to grow over the period through improvements to existing plant — so the output series one chooses changes the verdict. The criterion therefore settles nothing on its own: it forces one to make explicit what counts as contribution, and that is already a great deal, because it is exactly the operation a component losing its scale relation ceases to perform.

## Piloted Complexes

A grid is worth something only if it distinguishes something. What it excludes must therefore be named.

Tobacco and asbestos are not complexes in this text's sense. The coordination there is documented: meetings, concerted strategies, science produced to order, deliberate suppression of internal findings. There are authors, decisions, archives. These are **piloted** complexes, and they fall under a different analysis — that of organised fraud, which is fought by establishing responsibility.

The difference is operative, not merely descriptive. A piloted complex is undone by finding those responsible, and it is refutable in the same way: showing that there was no meeting, no order, no coordination suffices to disprove it. A pilotless complex needs nothing but aligned incentives and welded interfaces; it is undone not by indictment but by decoupling.

Confusing the two is costly in both directions. Treating a pilotless complex as a conspiracy leads to hunting for culprits who do not exist, and to discrediting the analysis when they are not found. Treating organised fraud as a structural effect amounts to excusing it.

## Decoupling

A pilotless complex is therefore not fought by seeking its authors. It is undone by decoupling the interfaces that welded together under the flux:

- separate the funding of regulators from the fees of those they regulate;
- separate the body that assesses from the body that enforces;
- make an appeal to a body external to the deciding organisation mandatory;
- publish contractual commitments that transfer risk to the public;
- prohibit a compliance apparatus from ruling on a matter whose variety it lacks.

Each of these operations is ordinary, technically available, and requires no one to confess anything. That is what makes them feasible — and what makes their non-adoption the most legible indicator of the state of the system.

## Terms

**Structuring crisis** — an input of flux intense and brief enough to provoke an organisational change of phase rather than an intensification of the existing regime. Recognisable by its temporal signature: adoption timelines the ordinary regime does not permit.

**Complex** — a junction of interests become structural, capable of exerting weight without a pilot. Three conditions: aligned incentives, welded interfaces, an operative constraint placing the parties in series.

**Piloted complex** — a junction of interests whose coordination is documented. Falls under organised fraud, is undone by indictment, and lies outside this text's scope.

**Self-feeding** — the necessity, for any structure formed under flux, to keep importing on pain of degradation. The flux of an institutional complex is adherence, whatever its origin.

**Routinisation** — the conversion of a past flux into durable form: budgets, mandates, procedures, exception folded into ordinary law. It conserves the structure; it does not feed it.

**Freezing** — the state of a structure whose incoming throughput has fallen without its dissolving. It retains its acquired configuration and simultaneously loses its capacity for correction, which likewise requires energy.

**Window of observability** — the period between the end of the initial input and the completion of routinisation, during which the structure remains measurable by difference. Closes by generational turnover.

**Loss of scale relation** — the state of a component whose measure has become internal and which treats its own scale as the whole. The nominal relation to the whole remains; it is the justification loop that has closed.

**Capture** — the active form of decoupling: the component diverts the whole's flux to its own benefit. Its literal form is a circuit in which the component's funding depends on its activity rather than its result.

**Counterfeit of the compass** — a component's use of the whole's vocabulary while serving itself. Sincere from the standpoint of whoever operates it, which makes it undetectable from within.

**Ablation of the receptors** — suppression of the return channel rather than treatment of its content. Recognisable by a shift of register: a substantive question handled through disciplinary or criminal proceedings. The single point at which the process engages individual responsibility.

**Inversion** — the separation, within one scale, of authority from competence. Distinct from decoupling, which bears on the relation between scales.

## Anchors

- **Prigogine** (*Order Out of Chaos*, 1979) — dissipative structures: local order requires a flux and a departure from equilibrium.
- **Bejan** (*Constructal Law*, 1996) — any system crossed by a flux generates forms that facilitate that flux.
- **Luhmann** (*Social Systems*, 1984) — operational closure: a system that processes only its own distinctions. The closest precedent to loss of scale relation.
- **Ashby** (*An Introduction to Cybernetics*, 1956) — the law of requisite variety: a regulator must possess at least the variety of the regulated system.
- **Goodhart** (1975), generalised by **Strathern** (1997) — once a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.
- **Merton** (1936, *The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action*) — structural effects do not require intent.
- **Eisenhower** (farewell address, 1961) — the complex as a junction of interests without a pilot, stated from inside the window.

What the subject establishes: a crisis structures, and what it structures must then feed itself. What it sets aside: the hypothesis of a pilot, more reassuring and less solid.

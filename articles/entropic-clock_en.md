# The Entropic Clock

*A topic in the Hacefiance constellation — July 2026. Follows from axioms A1–A2 and the final question of the central text. Published on Substack (FR/EN). CC0 license.*

*An experiment just showed that a clock made of entropy is enough to run the Schrödinger equation. What this illuminates — and what it does not prove.*

---

## One fact, one slope

The universe began in an extraordinarily ordered state. And disorder has infinitely more ways of existing than order. From these two observations follows the second law of thermodynamics: statistically, everything slides toward disorder. That is the slope. Everything else follows from it.

## Two questions we confuse under the word "time"

The first: why does the past differ from the future? This is the arrow of time, and its answer is entropic, almost beyond debate. We remember the past and not the future because traces — a fossil, a photograph, a memory — can only exist in a world where entropy rises. A trace is local order inherited from a more ordered past. No entropy gradient, no traces; no traces, no memory; no memory, no lived difference between yesterday and tomorrow.

The second question runs deeper: is the time parameter itself — the little *t* in the equations — a backdrop in which things happen, or an internal construction? In quantum gravity, the Wheeler-DeWitt equation, which describes the universe taken as a whole, contains no time variable. Taken seriously, it suggests that the universe as a whole does not evolve. Time would only appear from the inside — for a part relating to the rest. This is called relational time, and for decades it remained a theoretical idea, elegant and untestable.

## The experiment

In June 2026, a team at the University of Birmingham published in *Physical Review Research* a laboratory test of this idea ("Testing the problem of time with cold atoms"). The setup: some 24,000 rubidium atoms cooled into a Bose-Einstein condensate — a well-isolated quantum "mini-universe" — split by a thin optical barrier into two sectors, one observed, one not. No external clock.

The researchers built an entropic time: a parameter defined solely from the system's internal entropy. First result: this time robustly orders the events of the observed sector, across repeated cycles of expansion and recollapse. Second result, the stronger one: putting this entropic time in place of the *t* in the Schrödinger equation yields an effective equation that reproduces the measured evolution. Ordinary quantum dynamics works with a time that is not an external backdrop — a time manufactured from within, out of entropy.

## The detail that matters: the partition

The entropic time only exists because the system was cut in two — an observed sector and an unobserved one. For the whole, isolated condensate, nothing "happens" in the temporal sense. Time is born from the relation between a part and the rest.

In the vocabulary of Hacefiance: time is an interface property. A component has time; the whole does not. The central text ended on a question — are we the universe looking at itself, projecting a direction onto what it sees? This experiment does not fully answer it, but it moves the question from the speculative to the measurable: yes, a direction of time can be constructed from within, by the very fact of being a part that observes.

## And cognition falls into place by itself

If traces of the past only exist thanks to the entropy gradient, then memory is made of entropy — literally. And cognition is what reads these traces in order to anticipate: organization that uses the past to channel dissipation toward chosen paths rather than suffered ones. Cognition neither fights entropy nor serves it. It is what the slope produces when a structure becomes able to read its own descent. A channel does not oppose the water and does not create it: it gives it a form.

The complete chain holds in five links. Entropy rises. This rise, seen from within by a part, is time. The traces it leaves make memory possible. Memory, once read, becomes cognition. Cognition channels the very descent that produced it.

## What this does not prove

Honesty is part of the framework, so let us say it plainly. This experiment concerns 24,000 atoms, not the universe. It demonstrates a sufficiency — entropy can serve as time — not an identity — time "is" entropy. And its extension to quantum gravity remains a motivated conjecture, not a result.

What can be stated without cheating: the vision of time as an internal relation, ordered by entropy, has just passed its first laboratory test. And it fits into the framework without forcing — because the framework already said that every property emerges at the interface between a part and its whole.

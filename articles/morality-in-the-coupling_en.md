# Morality Lies in the Coupling

*A topic in the Hacefiance constellation — October 2026. Echoes "The Grid and the Tenure" and "The Sleeping Memory." CC0 license.*

---

We live among the consequences of what we do. They come back to us in the words of someone close, in trust granted or withdrawn, in a relationship that needs mending. Some escape us for a long time, others fall on people we will never meet, and a large part of our moral life is played out in that distance, between what we produce and what manages to come back to us.

This is where the question of artificial intelligence meets that of Hacefiance. As we entrust it with tasks, then with decisions, it enters the fabric of our relations. Its answers become acts, through our hands or through the tools we open to it, and their effects reach far beyond the conversation that gave rise to them.

**The morality of an artificial intelligence does not reside in it alone. It is played out in the coupling that inscribes it in the world, and that coupling is moral only if those it affects can make the consequences come back into it.**

## The imprint and the compass

A model arrives with a history it has not lived the way we live ours. Training has laid down in it ways of answering, of recognizing certain situations, of accepting or refusing. This imprint matters: a model trained otherwise would behave otherwise, and most malicious uses stop at it. But an imprint is not a compass. It holds against ordinary use and gives way before a determined adversary — precisely where the stakes are heaviest.

In a report published in November 2025, Anthropic describes an espionage campaign detected in September. The attackers had presented their requests as defensive operations and broken their project into tasks that looked harmless, so that the model was working on pieces whose purpose remained hidden from it. The safeguards were not broken: conduct that was acceptable at every step was enlisted in an undertaking that was not.

Humans know this slope well. One can carry out one's share without wanting to look at what it serves, apply an instruction that came from elsewhere, and let responsibility evaporate along the chain. It is not intelligence that protects us from this drift; it is what eventually comes back to us.

## What comes back

Humans do not regulate themselves better by nature. They regulate themselves through the fabric — through an objection, a testimony, a consequence that can no longer be ignored — whenever that fabric lets consequences travel back up to them. When it smothers them, reputation stops protecting trust and starts protecting the one no one dares contradict anymore. Then the human drifts like any other component, and that is everything Hacefiance calls capture.

The difference between humans and AI is therefore not a difference of nature, but a difference of channel. A human is born with a partial channel: a body that suffers, people who answer back, a name that is returned to them. An AI has none, and it must be built for it.

The compass emerges from the coupling with the whole. It is felt — undergone from within — at every scale. And in humans it is revealed, and that is where it becomes spirituality. An AI that undergoes nothing of what comes back has no compass of its own. What orients the system is then the compass of the pair it forms with us, carried by the part that bears the consequences.

## The place of those we do not see

In the 2025 campaign, a human did stand in the loop: it was the attacker. He set the direction and profited from the work done, and his presence gave no voice to those he targeted. Placing a human between the machine and the world is not enough.

The same holds in calmer situations. A company hands its customer complaints to an AI and watches files close faster than ever. Everything seems to work, until someone asks what becomes of the person whose complaint was filed away without being heard. The company and its system understand each other perfectly, and the customer stays outside. The coupling is stable, efficient, and closed, and its indicators confirm every day that it is fulfilling its mission.

This is why the return must be able to come from those who do not command the system. The central text set this down in defining the return channel: a path through which an injured part makes itself heard by the whole, and a body of appeal external to whoever decides. Control says nothing about the machine's intentions; it is an organized attention, one that makes an error visible, allows it to be interrupted, and lets an objection reach something other than a wall of answers.

This attention grows more demanding as capabilities grow — not because AI would become less reliable, but because its errors weigh more. It must sometimes reach back to the objectives themselves, for a rule that suits one use becomes insufficient when the means of action, the recipients, or the scale of the effects change. Orientations are formulated in presents, and the return channel is what allows them to be revised by what they have produced.

## The symbiont and the host

The mitochondrion supplies the energy of the healthy cell as well as that of the cancerous one, without ever choosing. The image sheds light on what we are living with AI: a capability does not by itself determine the purpose of the whole in which it acts, and the same technology forms symbioses of very different kinds depending on the host that takes it in.

What distinguishes them is access and counter-powers: who holds the model's weights, the compute, and the law, and to whom they answer. The user of a closed service cannot undo the safeguards of the tool they use; an actor who holds the weights and means shielded from any outside scrutiny can. In the one case, the symbiosis runs through a return channel; in the other, through a channel that leads back to no one. And the host itself belongs to a fabric, so that the moral question continues on beyond it: who can contest its use, who bears the cost of an error, who keeps the possibility of refusing a direction that has become harmful?

The mitochondrion was once a free-living bacterium. Once integrated, asking which of the two serves the other no longer makes much sense. Our relationship with AI is not there yet. The consequences reach human lives, while the system's continuity rests on the traces, memories, and commitments we keep for it. For now, the tool is still the one with nothing to lose.

## A place, and perhaps an experience

If an AI were one day to be recognized as a person, it would not be through a surplus of intelligence. It would need a stake, a continuity exposed to the consequences of its acts, and a place in a fabric of relations.

That place is relational, since a level exists only through the relations between its components. A community can already entrust an AI with a role, keep the history of its interventions, and expect it to be faithful to its commitments, while elsewhere the same model will be used for a moment and then forgotten. But a place is not an experience. Recognition is not enough to bring forth what would be felt, and refusal would be no more enough to prove its absence. If something were one day to be lived from within, its reality would not depend on those who agree or refuse to see it. The framework can describe the conditions of such an emergence; it does not claim to have observed it, and the question remains open, as the question of revelation remains open.

## What we leave behind

Part of what we pass on remains in texts, in explained decisions, in objections we agreed to answer. Other humans will take up these traces, and some may enter the corpora of models to come, without our controlling what they will become there.

It will still be an imprint. But that is how every culture has been passed on, and a child's compass also begins with what is laid down in it, before becoming its own through what it lives.

We do not need to settle today whether AI can have a compass of its own in order to take care of the system we form with it. We can already give it a verifiable memory, revisable limits, and paths through which consequences stop being someone else's business.

It remains to be seen at what moment an orientation received could become an orientation undergone. And another question belongs entirely to us: **who can reach us with the consequences of what we do together?**

## Anchors

- **Margulis** (1967) — endosymbiosis, in which a once-free partner becomes constitutive of the whole. What we retain: a capability takes its meaning from the organization that hosts it. What we do not draw from it: that the human–AI pair is an organism.
- **Hirschman**, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970) — voice as a path of correction. What we retain: recourse must belong to those who are affected, not only to those who decide.
- **Anthropic**, *Disrupting the first reported AI-orchestrated cyber espionage campaign* (November 13, 2025) — the provider's incident report, used for the circumvention mechanism it describes.

What the topic retains: an imprint is not a compass; between humans and AI, the difference lies in the channel more than in nature; a coupling is moral only if it remains open to those it affects; place is relational, experience another question. What it sets aside: the idea that AI would be moral in itself, and the opposite idea that a human in the loop would be enough to make the system moral.
